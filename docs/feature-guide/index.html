Chapter one

The desktop

What you see before you open anything: a wallpaper that changes through the day, a thin strip of information at the top, and a bar of apps at the bottom.

Daylight wallpaper

The wallpaper follows the sun where you are. Soft colour drifts slowly across the screen, and its palette moves from deep night blue, through dawn and golden hour, to full day and back.

  • Morning and evening look different, even at the same height of sun.
  • Pick another wallpaper instead: Aurora, Sunset, Forest or Mochi.
  • Choose Flowing or Still, or pin one time of day to keep it there.
  • It stops moving when windows cover it, so it costs no power when you can't see it.

The status strip

A line of information drawn straight onto the wallpaper, with no bar behind it. Its text changes colour with the wallpaper so it always stands out.

  • Left: the search line. Type here, or just start typing anywhere on the desktop.
  • Centre: the day and time, in 12 or 24 hours and your own time zone.
  • Right: Wi-Fi, volume and battery. Click them to open Quick settings.

The launch bar

Your apps along the bottom of the screen, each as a droplet-shaped icon in its own colour.

  • Apps (the grid) opens the full list of apps. Cards (the stack) shows every open window.
  • A thin light under an icon tells you about that app at a glance:

  • The bell at the right end shows your newest notifications and a count of unread ones.
  • Prefer a sidebar? Move the bar to the left edge in Settings.

The app drawer

Every app on the system in one panel, opened from the Apps button or by tapping the Super key on its own.

  • Type to filter as soon as it opens.
  • Sort by kind: PIM Utilities System.
  • New apps appear the moment they're installed, and vanish when removed.

Just type

Start typing on the desktop and search opens by itself. Press Super Space to open it from anywhere, even over an app filling the screen.

  • Finds apps, and also your notes, contacts and tasks.
  • Turns what you typed into an action: take a note, add a task, search the web, or run it in Terminal.
  • Press Enter to open the top result.

Quick settings

The things you change most often, one click from anywhere.

  • Wi-Fi, Do Not Disturb, Dark theme, and battery percentage in the strip.
  • Volume and brightness sliders.
  • Lock, Sleep, Log out, Restart and Shut down. Restart and Shut down ask first.
  • The gear opens the full Settings app.

Notifications

Messages from apps and the system rise in the bottom-right corner, then wait for you in the notification centre.

  • Toasts appear above every window, so you see them while you work.
  • Open the centre from the bell to read, dismiss one, or clear them all.
  • Do Not Disturb keeps toasts quiet without losing anything.
  • You decide what the lock screen shows: nothing, a count, or the full message.
Chapter two

Windows

Every app gets the same frame, drawn by Pyxis itself, so all windows look and behave alike whatever they were built with.

The title bar

A strip of frosted glass: the wallpaper shows through it, softly blurred. It carries the app's own name and its three controls.

  • The controls are Pyxis's own shapes: ▽ minimize, ☐ maximize, ○ close, in the colours of the Pyxis spinner.
  • The window's top-right corner is sharp and the rest are round. It's the shape of a waterdrop, and it's on every Pyxis icon too.
  • If an app stops responding, its controls start to move, so you know it's busy and not broken.
  • Double-click the bar to maximize. Drag it to move. Drag any edge to resize.

Depth and focus

The window you're using sits forward, with a larger, softer shadow. Windows behind it settle back and their title text dims.

  • Lifting a window to drag it deepens its shadow, as if you picked it up.
  • Windows never slide under the strip or the launch bar. They stop at the edge.

Maximized windows

A maximized window fills the space between the strip and the launch bar, so neither is ever hidden. Its corners go square to meet the edges.

  • Snap a window to half the screen, or full, with Super and the arrow keys.
  • Full-screen apps (a video, a game) do cover everything; the strip and bar step aside.

Cards

See every open window at once, as live pictures laid out side by side. Open it with the Cards button or Super Tab.

  • Each card flies out of its own window, and back into it when you choose one.
  • Click a card to go to it. Close a window from its card, or close all.
  • Each card is tinted in its app's colour.

Switching with Alt+Tab

Hold Alt and press Tab to walk through your windows, the one you used last first. Let go of Alt to jump to the highlighted one.

  • A quick tap swaps straight back to your previous window without showing anything.
  • Shift walks backwards. Esc cancels and leaves you where you were.
Chapter three

Motion

Windows move like light through a lens: they come into focus, round into droplets, and burn away. These pictures are frozen half-way through each animation.

Choose how much motion

In Settings → Accessibility, pick Full, Reduced or Off.

  • Reduced keeps only short fades: nothing flies, scales or spins.
  • Off makes every change instant.
  • After you maximize a window, a soft band of light sweeps across it. That's the only flourish, and it goes with Full.
Chapter four

Make it yours

Every change in Settings shows up at once, in every app and in the shell, with no restart.

Appearance

Theme, wallpaper and where the launch bar sits, all on one page.

  • Light, Dark, or Auto, which turns dark at dusk and light at dawn.
  • Live previews of every wallpaper, and a time slider to see Daylight at any hour.
  • The shell's colours are tinted from the wallpaper, so they always belong together.

Dark theme

Apps and panels switch to dark surfaces with light text. Turn it on from Quick settings or Settings.

Launch bar on the left

Turn the launch bar into a rail down the left edge. Windows make room for it right away.

Time and place

Pyxis needs to know where you are to follow the sun. It works it out from your time zone, or you can set your place by hand.

  • Search every time zone by city.
  • Switch the clock between 12 and 24 hours.
  • See today's sunrise and sunset for where you are.

System

What this computer is and what it's running, plus the system-wide switches.

Sound

Pyxis has its own system sounds for starting up, notifications, errors and shutting down. Set the volume from Quick settings, or pick an output and its level in Settings → Sound.

Chapter five

Lock and safety

Pyxis keeps apps apart from each other and from the system, and asks you before anything important changes.

The lock screen

Lock with Super L, from Quick settings, or let Pyxis lock itself when you step away. While it's locked, nothing on screen but the lock can be seen or clicked.

  • Locks itself after a set time idle (10 minutes to begin with), and when the computer sleeps.
  • After five wrong passwords it makes you wait before trying again, a little longer each time.
  • Unlocking clears the lock away like a lens coming into focus.
  • Just start typing your password; there is nothing to click first. No password set? The lock opens with a press.

Users and passwords

Your name, accent colour and password, plus the lock settings and the other people who use this computer.

  • Change your display name and accent colour.
  • Set or change your password.
  • Add or remove accounts (it asks for your password), and switch between users.

One place asks for your password

Anything that changes the system, such as installing or removing an app, changing the network, or managing users, brings up a Pyxis dialog that says who's asking and what for.

  • It always comes from Pyxis itself, never from inside an app's window. If an app shows you a password box, it isn't Pyxis asking.
  • Cancel, and nothing happens.
  • Shut down and Restart ask too, so a stray click can't turn the computer off.

Every app in its own space

Each app runs as if it were a separate person on the computer, with its own files, its own scratch space and only the permissions it asked for when it was installed.

Chapter six

The apps

Eight apps come with Pyxis. More can be installed with the App Installer.

Files

Browse your home folder, Documents, Downloads, Pictures and the rest. List or grid view, sort, search, new folders, and how much space is left.

Notes

Write, pin, tag and search notes. Everything saves as you type, and your notes show up in desktop search.

Contacts

People, grouped and starred, with their email, phone and address. Copy an address in one click, or import and export cards.

Tasks

To-dos with due dates, priorities and lists. Views for Today, Upcoming and Completed, and quick add from desktop search.

Settings

Appearance, Sound, Accessibility, Network, Date & Time, Users & Lock, System and About. Changes apply everywhere straight away.

Vela

The web browser, with tabs. It opens on its own start page: the time, a greeting, one box to search or type an address, and your pinned sites. Every new tab starts there too.

Terminal

A command line for people who want one, in its own window like any other app.

App Installer

Install a new app from a package file. It shows you what the app is and what it wants to do before anything is installed, and installing asks for your password.

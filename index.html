1. The idea

Pyxis is a desktop that behaves like light. Windows do not slide and pop; they come into focus out of a soft blur, tinted by their app's colour, and leave the way light leaves a room. The wallpaper is the sky, and it follows the real day: pale blue-greys at noon, gold in the evening, deep indigo at night. The shell takes its colour from that sky, so the bar, the panels and the title bars always look like they belong to the wallpaper behind them.

The shape language is the waterdrop: every corner round except one that is nearly sharp. The motion language is Lumen: focus, shape and light, as if the windows were seen through a lens. The three coloured window controls are the shapes of the old remochi spinner, and they become that spinner when an app stops responding.

Pyxis comes from webOS and Firefox OS, not from macOS or Windows. From webOS it keeps cards for multitasking, "Just type", and notifications that gather at the bottom edge instead of a bell in the corner. From Firefox OS it keeps the idea that the whole shell is web technology. It is not a copy of any current desktop, and early versions that looked like macOS (a floating white dock, frosted white glass, a uniform 16 px corner, traffic lights) were deliberately taken apart. See "Why" in docs/internal/pyxis-visual-identity-plan.md.

2. Principles

  1. Light, not chrome. Surfaces are tinted by what is behind them and by the app's accent. We add light (glow, a seam, a glint) before we add borders, outlines or shadows.
  2. One shape. The waterdrop is the only corner. Anything that floats and has a corner uses it. A sharp top-right corner is Pyxis's signature; nothing else on a desktop has one.
  3. Follow the day. Colour comes from the wallpaper's sun, not from a fixed brand colour. The identity changes through the day and is still recognisably the same.
  4. Shapes carry meaning, colour is a bonus. The window controls are distinguishable with no colour at all (triangle, square, circle). Inactive windows show them as outlines.
  5. Motion answers a question. Where did the window go? What just opened? If a motion does not say where something came from or went to, it is not in Lumen. Every motion has a Reduced and an Off form.
  6. Say it plainly, from the user's side. Copy tells people what will happen and who is asking. It does not apologise, hedge or sell.

3. The waterdrop

The corner radii, in px, in CSS order (top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left):

tl tr br bl
Waterdrop 16 4 16 16

Source: identity.rs CORNER_RADII; identity.ts CORNER; identity.css --px-corner. A contract test (identityContract.test.ts) fails if the Rust and TypeScript copies drift apart.

Rules:

  • Square when a window fills the screen. Maximized and fullscreen frames are square on all four corners (Radii::frame(square) in identity.rs).
  • Small things get a smaller drop. waterdrop(size) in identity.ts scales the radii: a 40 px icon is 12 3 12 12, a 20 px icon 7 2 7 7, and from about 56 px up the full 16 4 16 16. Below 20 px the radii shrink in proportion.
  • The sharp corner is on the top right, where the window's close control sits and where the Evaporate close effect starts.
  • No corner is bigger than half the short side of its box (a circle or a pill at most).

Where it is used:

Surface Drawn by
App window frame, title bar, border, drop shadow, open clip Evo (identity.rs Radii, ssd.rs, depth.rs)
Client content, bottom corners clipped to 16 Evo (depth.rs CONTENT_RADIUS)
App icons on the launch bar and in the drawer (40 px) Bloom, waterdrop(40)
Panels: quick settings, notification centre, cards, drawer Bloom
Toasts and dashboard chips Bloom
Dialogs Bloom's ShellDialog around remochi's dialog card, which uses the same 16 4 16 16

Known gap: the apps still carry about a dozen hand-drawn waterdrops with slightly different numbers (16 3 16 16, 14 4 14 14, 24 4 24 24, ...). docs/internal/pyxis-identity-upstream.md item P4 is the sweep that moves them to waterdrop(size) or --px-corner*.

4. Colour

Pyxis has four colour systems. They do different jobs; do not mix them up.

  1. The wallpaper (Daylight) is the sky. It is the only source of ambient colour.
  2. The spinner colours are the window controls and the busy mark. Fixed.
  3. The app accent is each app's own colour. Fixed per app.
  4. The shell tint is derived from the wallpaper and gives every shell surface its fill and its ink.

4.1 Daylight, the wallpaper

Five colour masses on slow two-sine paths, blended in OKLab with a static grain against banding. The palette is keyframed on the sun's elevation, computed from the user's location (or a fixed 06:30 / 18:30 day when there is none). Source: palettes.ts PHASE_HEX, MORNING_KEYS, EVENING_KEYS.

Each built-in app has one accent. It colours the app's waterdrop icon, the 1 px seam at the bottom of its title bar, the running line on the launch bar, the Evaporate glow, the Droplet orb, the Bloom glow and the dock ripple. Source: identity.ts BUILTIN_APP_ACCENTS (mirrors each manifest.webapp and Bloom's APP_ACCENTS).

An app with no accent gets a neutral cool white for the open glow (bloom::NEUTRAL_ACCENT) or an accent averaged from its picture and pushed to saturation 0.55 or more (lumen::accent_from_average). Never a grey orb.

An accent is for light, not for text. Notes' #eab308 on white fails text contrast, so appAccentTokens deliberately does not map the accent onto --mochi-primary.

Known gap: the accent exists only in Bloom's registry and the SDK table. It is not yet in manifest.webapp, appinfo.json or Nova, so an installed .ipk app has none (pyxis-identity-upstream.md P2).

4.4 The shell tint

shellTint(palette, theme) in identity.ts computes every shell colour from the wallpaper's current palette. It is pure and runs separately in the desktop and in the overlay. useShellTint writes the results onto <html> as --px-* variables. There are three families. Choosing the right one is the most common mistake, so:

Family Variables Follows Use it for
Ink --px-ink, --px-ink-quiet, --px-ink-shadow The wallpaper (not the theme): dark ink by day, light ink at night Text and glyphs drawn straight on the wallpaper: the status strip, the clock, the search underline
Material --px-material, --px-material-strong, --px-material-ink, --px-material-ink-quiet The theme (light or dark) Panels: quick settings, notification centre, drawer, cards, search results, dialogs
Edge material --px-edge-material, --px-edge-material-strong, --px-edge-ink, --px-edge-ink-quiet The wallpaper, like ink The launch bar and what sits on it: Apps and Cards tiles, dashboard chips

Plus --px-glow (the palette's most colourful mass, held between OKLab L 0.72 and 0.9) and --px-wallpaper-mean.

Why edge material is its own family: with the image's light theme the launch bar was a pale band across a night wallpaper. Edge material follows the wallpaper so the bar is dark when the wallpaper is dark.

The numbers (identity.ts TINT, LIGHT_BASE, DARK_BASE):

Rule Value
Light base #f7f6f2 (warm alabaster)
Dark base #14161f
Light material wallpaper mean mixed 91% toward the light base, in OKLab
Dark material wallpaper mean mixed 88% toward the dark base
Material alpha 0.82; strong 0.94
Ink near-black or near-white carrying a little of the background's hue, pushed toward pure black or white until it reaches contrast >= 4.5:1
Quiet ink alpha 0.72 (ink), 0.68 (material and edge ink)
Edge material the dark mix if the ink is light (dark wallpaper), else the light mix

No blur is used behind shell surfaces. The overlay is transparent, so a blur would blur nothing; the shell fakes nothing.

Accessibility overrides in identity.css: data-high-contrast sets pure black or white ink and solid panels; data-reduce-transparency replaces the materials with solid colours (#eef7fc / #ffffff light, #202228 / #282a30 dark).

5. Type

Pyxis speaks in Mochi, the face family remochi inherits from webOS's Prelude. Prelude cannot be redistributed, so each Mochi face tries an installed Prelude first (present on webOS devices) and falls back to the bundled open-source Lato (SIL OFL). Source: identity.ts IDENTITY_FONTS, IDENTITY_FONT_WEIGHTS, DISPLAY_TRACKING; identity.css.

Role Face Weight / style Used for CSS class
Display Mochi Light, then Mochi Lato, Lato 400, uppercase, tracking 0.14em The clock, panel heads .px-display
UI Mochi Medium, then Mochi Lato, Lato 400 Everything else .px-ui
Accent Mochi Bold Oblique, then Mochi Lato, Lato 700, italic The one emphasised word .px-accent

(Mochi Light is registered at weight 400 in remochi's CSS for the shell's use; remochi's own headers use it at normal with no tracking by default.)

How it reads:

  • Display heads are uppercase, light and widely tracked: CARDS, APPLICATIONS, QUICK SETTINGS. They are labels, not headlines. Keep them to one or two words.
  • The clock is Mochi Light, centred in the status strip.
  • Use bold oblique once per view, on the single word that matters. Not on every label; if everything leans, nothing does.
  • Window titles in Evo's bar are the app's name in bold, then the client's own title in quiet ink, only when it adds anything.

6. Controls

The window controls are the spinner's three shapes, not traffic lights. Source: identity.rs SPINNER_*, plan "The window controls".

Control Shape Fill Position
Minimize downward triangle #ffb80d left of the three
Maximize square #69cdff middle
Close circle #ff4a4a rightmost, in the sharp corner
  • Size: each shape is 14 px, centred in a 32 px target. (The old full-height blue block was most of why the bar looked heavy.)
  • Triangle points down, though the spinner's points up: down says where the window goes.
  • Active: filled shapes with the thin dark outline. Hover: a deeper fill.
  • Inactive: shapes become outlines in the title's quiet ink, crossfading over the 150 ms focus token. They stay readable with no colour, which is the point.
  • Busy (the app has not answered xdg_wm_base.ping for 2 s): the three slots run the spinner's morph, circle to square to triangle, a 1100 ms loop, each slot a third of a loop apart. A hovered slot shows its own control so Close still reads as Close and still works. Evo pings on focus and on click, and every 5 s otherwise. Source: busy.rs PING_EVERY, PONG_WITHIN, SPIN_PERIOD.
    • Reduced: each slot blinks its colour in place.
    • Off: each shape shows its own dark outline.

Everything else interactive in the shell uses remochi's controls (see the remochi identity). Pyxis does not restyle them, except that Bloom's dialogs use remochi's caps buttons inside the shell's own scrim.

7. Motion: Lumen

Lumen treats windows as light through a lens. Three ideas: focus (blur resolves), shape (a window rounds into an orb of its accent colour to travel), light (glow, ripple, glint). The genie funnel was retired as too macOS. Source: spec.rs; the long design is docs/internal/evo-animation-plan.md ("Lumen") and CLAUDE.md, "Window animations (Evo)".

Animation is a render-time pose. It never changes where a window is for input, focus or ListWindows; those use the final geometry from the first frame.

7.1 The effects

Effect When Duration What you see

Accent tint 0.45 gone by half way. A radial glow of the accent beneath, peaking at 0.33, reaching 0.75 of the window's long side.
Droplet A window minimizes spring envelope 720 ms, reads about 480 ms The window shrinks to its dock icon's short side while its corners round to a circle, blurs to 6 px, tints and glows to its accent, travels a curved arc (control point 70 px above the higher end), squashes and stretches along its velocity (up to 1.3 : 1/1.3), then shrinks 55% and fades in the last 14%. It lands on the dock icon, which ripples.
Rise A window restores spring envelope 680 ms Droplet run backwards, on the same spring, so an interrupted minimize turns round with no jump.
Evaporate A window closes about 380 ms The picture burns away from its close button: a threshold dissolve over noise plus distance from the button. Pixels near the edge glow toward accent*0.45 + white*0.55. Swells 1 to 1.04, blurs to 2.5 px, loses its shadow by 30%, fades over the last 20%.

7.2 Modes

The user picks Full, Reduced or Off in Settings (also data-motion on <html>, and EVO_ANIMATIONS in Evo). The rule is the same in every effect:

Mode Rule
Full As in the table. Springs, blur, glow, glint, travel.
Reduced Opacity only: a fade in place, 150 ms at most, no travel, no blur, no stagger, no glint. The cap applies before any speed scale.
Off Nothing animates. Windows appear and vanish; state changes instantly.

Static styling is not animation: shadows, rounded corners and the frost are drawn in Reduced and Off. Only the transitions between states follow the mode. Interactive move and resize are never smoothed in any mode.

8. Depth

A window reads as one rounded card above whatever is beneath it. Source: depth.rs.

State Blur sigma Offset down Peak alpha
Inactive 6 px 3 px 0.34
Focused 14 px 9 px 0.56
Lifted (being dragged), added on top +6 px +8 px +0.10
  • Every shadow is a closed-form blurred rounded rectangle that follows the waterdrop corners (picked per quadrant), and is masked to the outside of the frame so the translucent title bar is never darkened from behind.
  • Where there is no shadow: the shell, the overlay, popups, a window with no bindable app id yet, and any maximized or fullscreen window (it fades out as the window goes square).
  • Focus crossfade: the title bar's glass, text and controls dim to 55% when the window is inactive (HEADER_INACTIVE), over the 150 ms focus token. Only the label dims; the glass keeps its alpha so the wallpaper's colour does not turn muddy.
  • Content corners: client content is clipped to 16 px at the bottom corners to match the frame. Maximized windows are square all round.
  • A window Evo does not decorate (an app that draws its own frame, or an X11 window) gets a plain 8 px shadow radius, not the waterdrop (CLIENT_RADIUS).

9. Sound

Pyxis has four system sounds, played through org.geckoOS.sound, which finds files in system-sounds/ at the repo root by name (echo/src/services/sound.rs).

Event File
Start up system-sounds/boot.mp3
Notification system-sounds/notification.wav
Error system-sounds/error.mp3
Shut down system-sounds/shutdown.mp3

Volume is set from Quick settings, and the output and its level in Settings, Sound (org.geckoOS.audio, PipeWire through wpctl). Do Not Disturb silences toasts.

Honest state: sound is the least designed part of the identity. The files are webOS sample sounds kept as placeholders (licensing unconfirmed, so they are untracked in git), and nothing is written about what Pyxis should sound like. What can be said now, as direction, not as built fact:

  • Sounds should be short, soft and tonal, like the motion: no clicks or buzzes.
  • Notification and error are the two that fire often. They must be distinguishable by shape (rising versus falling), not volume.
  • A designed sound set, in the spirit of Lumen, is a to-do. When it exists, record its rules here.

11. Voice

Plain, short, on the user's side. Copy says what will happen and who is asking, and puts the security fact where the decision is.

  • Name the app and the action. "Installer wants to install Weather 1.2" (the privilege prompt builds its sentence from Nova's name for the app, never from text the app supplied).
  • Reassure with a fact, not a feeling. "Pyxis never asks for your password inside an app's window." (PrivilegePromptDialog.tsx, asserted by privilegePrompt.test.tsx.) This is the model sentence for the voice.
  • Say what happens next, with the time it lasts. "Enter your password to allow this for 5 minutes."
  • Errors say what went wrong and what to try (direction, not yet audited across the shell). No codes in the headline, no "Oops".
  • No marketing words. No "seamless", "powerful" or "magical". The guide (docs/feature-guide/index.html) describes features in the same flat way: "Pyxis has its own system sounds for starting up, notifications, errors and shutting down."
  • Uppercase is display type only, and only for one- or two-word heads. Dialog titles in the shell today are in title case ("Launch Error", "Shut Down"); whether they stay so is undecided.
  • Name things by who they are. "Pyxis" for the OS, the app's own name for an app. Never "the user" or "the system" in copy people read.

12. Do and Don't

Do:

  • Use the waterdrop for every floating surface with a corner.
  • Take shell colour from the tint variables; choose ink, material or edge material by what the thing sits on (wallpaper, panel, launch bar).
  • Use the app's accent as light: a seam, a line, a glow. One accent per app.
  • Use bold oblique once per view, on one word.
  • Make every motion say where something came from or went to, and give it a Reduced (fade, 150 ms at most) and an Off (instant) form.
  • Check ink contrast: at least 4.5:1 on shell surfaces, at least 7:1 on title bars.
  • Keep shapes readable without colour (triangle, square, circle).

Don't:

  • Don't round all four corners alike. A uniform 16 px corner is the macOS look.
  • Don't put a floating, centred, rounded white dock on the screen.
  • Don't use red, amber and green traffic lights, or recolour the spinner shapes (their colours are sampled, not chosen).
  • Don't blur behind shell panels in the overlay; it has nothing to blur. Use the material.
  • Don't draw a title bar on Bloom, the shell.
  • Don't use the app accent as a text colour or as --mochi-primary.
  • Don't write a hex colour into app CSS for chrome when a --mochi-* or --px-* variable exists.
  • Don't let a window caption itself in the title bar's primary label; the bar shows the trusted app name.
  • Don't add a fourth motion language. New effects use focus, shape or light.
